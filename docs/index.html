M14t Touch

Touch and stylus input for the Lenovo ThinkVision M14t, on macOS. No kernel extension, no dependencies, no subscription.

macOS 13 or laterApple Silicon & IntelMIT licensed

Download Settings guide Source on GitHub

macOS recognises the M14t as a display and silently ignores its touch interface: the screen works, touch does nothing. The commercial driver for it costs about $171 for one licence. This does the same job, reads the panel's HID stream directly, and translates it into ordinary macOS input.

What it does

Touch

Tap, scroll and long-press to drag. A flick keeps going and slows to a stop, and scrolling rubber-bands at the end of a page the way a trackpad does.

Two fingers to zoom

Spread or close two fingers. The window under them comes forward first, so what you are touching is what zooms.

Secondary click

Rest a finger on something and tap with a second. The click lands where the first finger is — it is already pointing at the thing.

Three fingers for every window

Swipe up with three fingers to open Mission Control, then tap the window you want.

Stylus

Hovering moves the pointer, the tip draws, and both barrel buttons are configurable — including while the pen is only hovering. Pressure can be sent to applications that understand it.

A dot or a crosshair

Optionally the pen carries a small coloured dot — or a crosshair, whose empty centre suits aiming at something small — and the system arrow is hidden.

Calibration

Guided, four corners, saved per display — so a panel that is unplugged and plugged back in is remembered.

Palm rejection

Touches that begin while the pen is near the panel are ignored, so a hand resting on the screen does not interfere.

Diagnostics

A live view of what the panel is reporting, circles drawn wherever it sees a finger, a log you can read back, and a button that releases and retakes the panel when something goes odd.

Installing

  1. Download the disk image and drag M14t Touch into Applications.
  2. macOS will refuse to open it the first time, because the app is not notarized yet. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to the Security section, and choose Open Anyway. Or, in Terminal: 
    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/M14t Touch.app"
  3. Grant two permissions, both in Privacy & Security: Input Monitoring, so the app can read the panel, and Accessibility, so it can move the pointer and click.
  4. Quit the app and open it again — macOS only applies those permissions to a newly started process.
The app lives in the menu bar. If the icon is dim, the menu says what is missing — a disconnected panel, or a permission that was not granted.

Honest limitations

Things worth knowing before you install, rather than after.

The pen needs the app running The panel has to be taken exclusively, or macOS moves the pointer relative to wherever it already was and the pen fights it. The cost is that the pen does nothing at all while the app is not running.
Zoom is stepped A true pinch cannot be sent by any application without private APIs. Spreading your fingers sends ⌘= instead, which is a whole zoom level at a time rather than a smooth scale.
One three-finger gesture, not four A trackpad also switches desktops and shows an application's windows. Those run macOS's own shortcuts, and those shortcuts do not answer a synthesised keystroke — so they are not offered rather than offered and silently doing nothing.
Pressure is off by default It works — applications do receive it — but marking events as coming from a tablet changed behaviour badly in one browser-based drawing app, so it is a switch you turn on rather than something assumed.
An eraser stroke cannot be advertised The pen's near button really is an eraser in hardware, but the event that would tell applications so never reaches them. It can be mapped to an ordinary stroke instead.
Not notarized Hence the manual step above. It needs a paid Apple developer certificate, which this project does not have yet.
